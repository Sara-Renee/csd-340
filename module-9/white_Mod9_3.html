The Birth of Venus

Here is a piece I wrote for a creative writing class in 2024. The assignment was to write a short, firt-person narrative based on a famous painting. My story was based on The Birth of Venus, by Alexandre Cabanel (1863).

And so I’m awake and I cannot recall I was ever a girl. How I can speak this is a mystery to me, but I will tell you what I know.

The ocean is dark and violently quiet. _______ and primeval, like a dead-eyed shark, it thrashes you in its jaws without remorse or cruelty. Cold, black indifferent womb–I saw darkness and felt it crush me awake. I choked out a scream that made no sound and the pressure lifted. A vortex all around me, green and gray and blue, with white, foaming spiral arms threw me upward. The top of the ocean broke open and there above me was a pale blue _______ and a blinding light. I burned inside the white-hot gaze of the _______. Ancient, too, but awake and mean, the surface feels remorse and is cruel anyway. “Why am I here?” I asked the sun and it seared me with memories and histories and burdens. I learned I have a purpose against my will and let a wave roll me toward the shore.

So now I am cradled by the soft rhythm of this gentle wave, rocking me slowly where the sky meets the sea. My eyes are sleepy and my heart is heavy. I am cool water and I am made to extinguish angry fires. When the fires of this world meet me I suppose they might try to turn me into vapor. When I hold them I will become a hot spring and they will forget for a while how they hated me and hated themselves and wanted only blood. When I turn cool again, will they say I tricked them? Will they say I tempted them into forgetting themselves? If they do, I will make myself a _______ and I will find other fires to swallow.
